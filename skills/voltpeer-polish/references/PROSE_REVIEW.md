# Direct scientific prose and candidate review

## Edit for the reader

Give a paragraph a recognizable job. Start with its subject, comparison, method, or measured result instead of a routine announcement. In a reviewer response, lead with the answer or action and place the location where it helps the reader find the change. Delete repetition within and between passages. Retain qualifiers that delimit a measurement, an inferred mechanism, the comparison population, or an unresolved condition. Keep exact author wording when the user designates it.

Read adjacent paragraphs after a bulk edit. Look for consecutive stock openings, repeated caveat sentences, inflated evaluative words, and generic conclusions that could appear in any paper. Repair the logical connection or name the particular result. A compact, qualified conclusion usually reads better than a string of hedges. For a claim of change, preserve the baseline and the named endpoint.

## Check another editor's candidate

Compare the candidate to the same baseline at the same location. Use word count or text matching to catch omissions and duplicated edits. Inspect changes in agency, causation, modality, negation, quantities, numerical bounds, and citations separately. Preserve the following distinctions when they affect meaning.

| Evidence in the baseline | What the revised sentence must convey |
| --- | --- |
| Fitted spectral component area | The fitted signal, denominator, and any assumption needed to infer populations |
| Simulated coordination number | The model, counted species, and normalization |
| Reported pouch performance | The operating conditions and measurement basis for that cell and test branch |
| Indexed search terms | The searched fields, denominator, and limits of the retrieval |
| Proposed mechanism or design | The hypothesis and how it could be tested |

Accept proposed language that preserves meaning. Correct proposals that change it beyond the source. Mark evidence you could not verify. Keep native formatting and field links when editing a structured file, and spot-check the actual rendered wording after merging.

## Invented editing examples

| Baseline | Candidate to deliver | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It should be noted that the coated electrode retained 82% of its initial capacity after 100 cycles at 25 °C in the coin-cell test. | In the coin cell at 25 °C, the coated electrode retained 82% of its initial capacity after 100 cycles. | Removes the opener and preserves the baseline and configuration. |
| The fitted shoulder gained area, consistent with stronger ion association under these conditions. | The fitted shoulder gained area, consistent with stronger ion association under these conditions. | Keep the evidence grade when an editor proposes “proved stronger ion association.” |
| We agree with the reviewer and have updated the results section to clarify the comparator. The reference electrode used the same areal loading. | The reference electrode used the same areal loading. We added that condition to the comparison in Section 3.2. | Answers the concern before directing the reader to the revision. |
